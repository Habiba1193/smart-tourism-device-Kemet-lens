SHORT DESCRIPTION
Tutankhamun was a young pharaoh of ancient Egypt’s 18th Dynasty. He became world-famous after his almost intact tomb was discovered in the Valley of the Kings in 1922. His golden funerary mask is one of the most recognizable symbols of ancient Egypt.

OVERVIEW
Tutankhamun, also known as King Tut, was an Egyptian pharaoh from the New Kingdom period. He ruled during the late 18th Dynasty. Although his reign was short and he was not one of Egypt’s most powerful rulers, he became one of the most famous pharaohs in modern history because his tomb was discovered in a nearly intact condition.

His tomb contained thousands of objects, including coffins, jewelry, chariots, statues, furniture, weapons, clothing, ritual objects, and the famous golden mask. These objects gave archaeologists and historians a rare view of ancient Egyptian royal burial customs, craftsmanship, religion, kingship, and beliefs about the afterlife.

Tutankhamun is especially important because his burial collection preserved many details about how ancient Egyptians prepared a king for eternal life. His story connects politics, religion, archaeology, art, and funerary traditions.

HISTORICAL BACKGROUND
Tutankhamun lived during one of the most complex periods in ancient Egyptian history. Before his reign, Pharaoh Akhenaten promoted the worship of Aten, the solar disk, and reduced the role of traditional gods such as Amun. This period is often called the Amarna Period.

Tutankhamun was originally named Tutankhaten, a name connected to Aten. Later, his name was changed to Tutankhamun, showing Egypt’s return to the traditional worship of Amun and the older religious system.

This change was very important because it symbolized a restoration of traditional Egyptian religion after Akhenaten’s reforms. During Tutankhamun’s reign, temples connected with Amun and other traditional gods were restored, and the royal court moved away from the religious changes of the Amarna Period.

Because Tutankhamun became king as a child, many political and religious decisions were probably influenced by powerful advisors, priests, and officials. Important figures connected with his reign included Ay, a senior official who later became pharaoh, and Horemheb, a military commander who also later became pharaoh.

APPROXIMATE DATE
Tutankhamun ruled during the 14th century BCE, commonly around 1332–1323 BCE. Some chronologies may give slightly different dates, which is normal in ancient Egyptian history.

FAMILY AND SUCCESSION
Tutankhamun belonged to the royal family of the late 18th Dynasty. His father is commonly identified as Akhenaten. His wife was Ankhesenamun, who was also connected to the Amarna royal family.

Tutankhamun had no surviving heir. After his death, Ay became pharaoh, followed later by Horemheb. These successions show that the end of the 18th Dynasty was a period of political transition.

WHY HE IS CALLED THE BOY KING
Tutankhamun is called the Boy King because he became pharaoh when he was still a child, probably around 8 or 9 years old. He also died very young, around 18 or 19 years old. His young age made his story memorable, especially after the discovery of his richly furnished tomb.

REIGN AND RULE
Tutankhamun’s reign was short, lasting about nine years. He is not mainly remembered for military campaigns or large building projects like Ramses II. Instead, his reign is important because it represented a return to traditional religious practices after the Amarna Period.

His rule helped restore the worship of Amun and other traditional gods. This restoration was important for Egyptian society because religion was strongly connected to government, temples, economy, and royal authority.

TOMB
Tutankhamun’s tomb is known as KV62. It is located in the Valley of the Kings near Luxor. The tomb was discovered in 1922 by Howard Carter’s excavation team. It became famous because it was found in a nearly intact state, unlike many royal tombs that had been heavily robbed in antiquity.

The tomb was not very large compared with other royal tombs, but it contained an extraordinary collection of burial objects. Its smaller size may suggest that Tutankhamun died suddenly and that burial preparations were completed quickly.

TOMB DISCOVERY
Howard Carter’s team discovered the first step leading to the tomb entrance on 4 November 1922. The discovery became one of the greatest archaeological events of the 20th century. When Carter looked inside the tomb, he found a room filled with ancient objects.

The discovery captured worldwide attention and created a strong modern interest in ancient Egypt, sometimes called Egyptomania. Tutankhamun became a global symbol of ancient Egyptian civilization.

DISCOVERY TIMELINE
- c. 14th century BCE: Tutankhamun rules Egypt during the 18th Dynasty.
- c. 1320s BCE: Tutankhamun dies young and is buried in tomb KV62.

- Late November 1922: Howard Carter examines the sealed tomb entrance.
- 1923: The burial chamber is opened.
- 1925: The innermost coffin is opened and the golden mask is revealed.
- Modern period: Tutankhamun becomes one of the most famous symbols of ancient Egypt.

THE GOLDEN MASK
Tutankhamun’s golden funerary mask is his most famous artifact. It covered the head and shoulders of the mummy. The mask shows the king in an idealized divine form and was designed to protect him in the afterlife.

The mask is made mainly of gold and decorated with colorful inlays. Gold was strongly associated with divine power, eternity, and the flesh of the gods. The mask connects Tutankhamun with royal authority, divine kingship, and the hope of rebirth after death.

WHY THE TOMB WAS SO IMPORTANT
Tutankhamun’s tomb was important because it preserved one of the richest collections of ancient Egyptian royal burial objects ever discovered. Most royal tombs in the Valley of the Kings were robbed in ancient times, but Tutankhamun’s tomb still contained many treasures.

The discovery helped historians understand:
- Ancient Egyptian burial rituals.
- Royal funerary art.
- The role of gold and luxury materials.
- The belief in the afterlife.
- How Egyptian kings were prepared for eternity.
- The craftsmanship of ancient Egyptian artisans.
- Daily-life objects used by royalty.
- Religious symbols connected with death and rebirth.

DEATH AND HEALTH
Tutankhamun died young, around 18 or 19 years old. The exact cause of his death is still debated. Studies of his mummy have suggested possible health problems, injuries, or genetic conditions, but there is no single confirmed explanation.

For a simple museum explanation, it is best to say that Tutankhamun died unexpectedly at a young age. This may explain why his tomb was smaller than many other royal tombs and why some burial preparations may have been hurried.

LOCATION
Tutankhamun’s tomb, KV62, is located in the Valley of the Kings near Luxor, Egypt. Many objects from his tomb are associated today with major Egyptian museum collections, especially the Grand Egyptian Museum, where the Tutankhamun collection is a central attraction.

FAMOUS FOR
Tutankhamun is famous for:
- Being the Boy King.
- His golden funerary mask.
- His nearly intact tomb.
- The discovery of KV62 in 1922.
- The thousands of treasures found in his burial.
- Helping the modern world understand ancient Egyptian royal funerary customs.

WHY IT MATTERS
Tutankhamun matters because his tomb gave the world one of the clearest views of ancient Egyptian royal burial. His life was short, but his burial collection helped historians understand Egyptian beliefs about kingship, death, resurrection, divine protection, luxury, craftsmanship, and the afterlife.

Tutankhamun is important not because he was the most powerful pharaoh, but because his tomb preserved a unique collection of royal objects. These objects made him one of the best-known pharaohs in the world.
