SHORT DESCRIPTION
Ramses II was one of the most powerful and famous pharaohs of ancient Egypt. He was the third king of the 19th Dynasty and ruled for about 66 years, from 1279 to 1213 BCE. He is famous for his long reign, military campaigns, huge building projects, colossal statues, and temples such as Abu Simbel.

OVERVIEW
Ramses II, also called Ramses the Great, ruled Egypt during the New Kingdom, one of the most powerful periods of ancient Egyptian history. His reign was one of the longest in Egyptian history. He used architecture, statues, inscriptions, and military records to present himself as a strong and divine ruler.

He is strongly associated with large monuments across Egypt and Nubia. These monuments were not only religious buildings; they were also political messages showing the power of the king, the strength of Egypt, and the connection between the pharaoh and the gods.

HISTORICAL BACKGROUND
Ramses II came from the 19th Dynasty, after Egypt had returned to political and religious stability following the Amarna Period. His father, Seti I, was a strong king who restored Egyptian authority in parts of the Near East. Ramses II inherited a powerful kingdom and expanded his image as a warrior, builder, and divine ruler.

During his reign, Egypt competed with the Hittite Empire for influence in Syria and the Levant. The most famous event from this conflict was the Battle of Kadesh, fought early in Ramses II’s reign. Egyptian inscriptions presented the battle as a great personal victory for Ramses, although historically it was more complex and probably ended without a decisive military winner.

APPROXIMATE DATE
Ramses II ruled from 1279 to 1213 BCE, during the 13th century BCE.

FAMOUS FOR
Ramses II is famous for being one of Egypt’s longest-reigning pharaohs, his title Ramses the Great, the Battle of Kadesh, the Egyptian-Hittite peace treaty, the temples of Abu Simbel, the Ramesseum, Pi-Ramesses, colossal statues, and royal propaganda through inscriptions and monuments.

LOCATION
Ramses II is connected with many important sites, including Abu Simbel, Luxor Temple, Karnak, the Ramesseum, Pi-Ramesses, Memphis, the Valley of the Kings, and Abydos.

ABU SIMBEL
Abu Simbel is one of the most famous monuments of Ramses II. It is a complex of two rock-cut temples in southern Egypt, near Nubia. The Great Temple is famous for the four huge seated statues of Ramses II at the entrance. The smaller temple was dedicated to Queen Nefertari and the goddess Hathor.

COLOSSAL STATUES
Ramses II is strongly associated with colossal statues. These statues were used to express royal power, divine authority, stability, and eternal kingship. Important visual features include the nemes headdress, false beard, cartouche, seated posture, standing posture, and smaller family figures near the king’s legs.

BATTLE OF KADESH
The Battle of Kadesh was fought between Ramses II and the Hittites near the city of Kadesh in Syria. Egyptian texts describe Ramses as personally heroic, fighting bravely when his army was in danger. Modern historians usually understand the battle as politically complicated rather than a simple Egyptian victory.

EGYPTIAN-HITTITE PEACE TREATY
After years of conflict between Egypt and the Hittites, Ramses II’s reign became associated with one of the earliest known international peace treaties. The treaty helped create a diplomatic relationship between Egypt and the Hittite Empire.

BUILDING PROJECTS
Ramses II was one of the greatest builders in Egyptian history. His building work included Abu Simbel, the Ramesseum, Pi-Ramesses, additions to Luxor Temple, inscriptions at Karnak, colossal statues, and monuments in Nubia.

FAMILY
Ramses II’s father was Seti I and his mother was Queen Tuya. His most famous wife was Queen Nefertari. Another important wife was Isetnofret. Some important children include Khaemwaset, Merneptah, Bintanath, and Meritamen. Ramses II lived so long that many of his sons died before him, and he was eventually succeeded by Merneptah.

CONNECTION TO NEFERTARI
Queen Nefertari was Ramses II’s most famous wife. The Small Temple at Abu Simbel was dedicated to Nefertari and the goddess Hathor, showing her high royal status. Her tomb, QV66 in the Valley of the Queens, is one of the most beautiful tombs in Egypt.

RELIGION AND DIVINE KINGSHIP
Like other pharaohs, Ramses II presented himself as chosen by the gods and responsible for maintaining Ma’at, the ancient Egyptian idea of truth, balance, order, and justice. His monuments connect him with major gods such as Amun-Re, Ra-Horakhty, Ptah, Hathor, and Osiris.

SYMBOLISM
Ramses II symbolizes strong kingship, military power, divine authority, monumental architecture, stability, long rule, Egyptian power in Nubia and the Levant, royal propaganda, and protection of temples and sacred spaces.

MATERIALS ASSOCIATED WITH ARTIFACTS
Ramses II’s statues, temples, and objects may be associated with granite, sandstone, limestone, quartzite, gold, wood, faience, and pigments.

ICONOGRAPHY AND APPEARANCE
Ramses II is usually shown in a strong, idealized royal form. He may appear wearing the nemes headdress, the double crown, the blue war crown, or a false beard. He may be seated on a throne, standing in a formal royal pose, smiting enemies, offering to gods, or shown with cartouches containing his royal names.

WHY IT MATTERS
Ramses II matters because he represents the height of Egyptian royal monumentality and power during the New Kingdom. His temples, statues, inscriptions, and diplomatic records shaped how later generations understood Egyptian kingship.

HISTORICAL TIMELINE
- c. 1303 BCE – Ramses II is born, according to common historical estimates.
- Reign of Seti I – Ramses becomes crown prince.
- 1279 BCE – Ramses II becomes pharaoh.
- Early reign – Begins major military campaigns and building projects.
- c. 1274 BCE – Battle of Kadesh against the Hittites.
- 13th century BCE – Abu Simbel and other monuments are built.
- Later reign – Peace treaty and diplomatic relations with the Hittites.
- 1213 BCE – Ramses II dies after about 66 years of rule.
- Later history – His mummy is moved for protection and later found in a royal cache.
- Modern period – Ramses II becomes one of the most famous pharaohs in the world.

DID YOU KNOW?
Ramses II ruled so long that many Egyptians at the end of his reign may have never known another pharaoh.

HIDDEN DETAIL
On many statues of Ramses II, his royal name appears inside an oval shape called a cartouche. A cartouche protected and highlighted the king’s name.
