SHORT DESCRIPTION
Anubis was an ancient Egyptian god connected with mummification, funerary rituals, protection of the dead, and the journey to the afterlife. He is usually shown as a jackal or as a man with a jackal head.

OVERVIEW
Anubis was one of the most important funerary deities in ancient Egypt. His image appears in tombs, funerary texts, and objects connected with burial. He was believed to protect the dead, guide souls, and help with rituals of mummification. His jackal form may be connected with desert animals seen near cemeteries, transformed in Egyptian belief into a protective divine figure.

HISTORICAL BACKGROUND
In early periods of ancient Egyptian religion, Anubis held an important role as a lord and protector of the dead. Later, Osiris became more central as ruler of the afterlife, but Anubis remained important as the god associated with embalming, tomb protection, and the weighing of the heart. Priests performing mummification rituals could wear masks representing Anubis.

ERA
Ancient Egyptian religion across many periods

DYNASTY
Not limited to one dynasty; Anubis was worshiped throughout ancient Egyptian history.

APPROXIMATE DATE
Anubis appears in ancient Egyptian religion from early periods and remains important through later Egyptian history.

MATERIALS ASSOCIATED WITH ARTIFACTS
Anubis statues and images can be made or represented in wood, stone, painted plaster, metal, and tomb paintings.

FAMOUS FOR
Anubis is famous for his jackal-headed form, connection to mummification, protection of tombs, and role in the afterlife.

LOCATION
Images of Anubis are found in tombs, temples, funerary objects, and museum collections connected with ancient Egypt.

SYMBOLISM
Anubis symbolizes protection, mummification, death, transition, judgment, and safe passage to the afterlife. His black color in many images can symbolize regeneration, fertility of the Nile soil, and the transformation associated with death and rebirth.

WHY IT MATTERS
Anubis matters because he helps explain ancient Egyptian beliefs about death and the afterlife. His role shows that death was not viewed only as an ending, but as a carefully guided journey requiring rituals, protection, and divine support.

HISTORICAL TIMELINE
- Early Egyptian history – Anubis appears as a major funerary god.
- Old Kingdom – Anubis holds an important position connected with the dead.
- Later periods – Osiris becomes more central as ruler of the afterlife, while Anubis remains important in mummification and tomb protection.
- New Kingdom and later – Anubis appears widely in funerary art and texts.

DID YOU KNOW?
Anubis is often shown with a jackal head because jackals were associated with desert areas and cemeteries. Egyptian religion transformed this image into a powerful protector of the dead.

HIDDEN DETAIL
In the weighing of the heart scene, Anubis is often shown guiding or supervising the weighing of the deceased person’s heart against the feather of Ma’at, the symbol of truth and balance.

FUN FACTS
- Anubis is one of the most recognizable Egyptian gods.
- He is often shown in black, a color connected with regeneration and the afterlife.
- His image is popular in museums because it clearly represents ancient Egyptian funerary beliefs.
