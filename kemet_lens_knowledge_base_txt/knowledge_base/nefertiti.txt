SHORT DESCRIPTION
Nefertiti was an ancient Egyptian queen of the 18th Dynasty and the Great Royal Wife of Pharaoh Akhenaten. She is strongly associated with the Amarna Period and is famous today for her elegant painted bust.

OVERVIEW
Nefertiti is one of the best-known queens of ancient Egypt. Her name is commonly translated as “a beautiful woman has come.” She played a major role in the royal image of the Amarna Period, appearing in art beside Akhenaten and taking part in religious scenes connected with the sun disk Aten. Her famous bust is admired for its symmetry, color, and refined artistic style.

HISTORICAL BACKGROUND
Nefertiti lived during the 18th Dynasty, a period of major political, artistic, and religious change. She was the wife of Akhenaten, who promoted the worship of Aten and moved the royal court to a new city at Akhetaten, now known as Amarna. Art from this period often shows the royal family in a distinctive style, with more intimate family scenes and strong emphasis on Aten’s rays.

ERA

APPROXIMATE DATE
Flourished in the 14th century BC, during the reign of Akhenaten.

MATERIALS ASSOCIATED WITH ARTIFACTS
The famous Nefertiti bust is made of limestone covered with painted plaster.

FAMOUS FOR
Nefertiti is famous for her painted bust, her royal beauty, her role beside Akhenaten, and her connection to the Amarna religious transformation.

LOCATION
The famous bust was found at Amarna, in the workshop associated with the sculptor Thutmose. It is now held in Berlin.

SYMBOLISM
Nefertiti represents royal elegance, queenship, religious change, and the distinctive art of the Amarna Period. Her image also symbolizes the public role of royal women in ancient Egyptian religious and political life.

WHY IT MATTERS
Nefertiti matters because she shows that queens could have powerful public and religious roles in ancient Egypt. Her image helps explain the artistic and religious changes of the Amarna Period. Her bust is also one of the most recognized works of ancient Egyptian art.

HISTORICAL TIMELINE
- c. 1353 BC – Akhenaten begins his reign, and Nefertiti becomes central to royal imagery.
- Amarna Period – Nefertiti appears prominently in scenes connected with Aten worship.
- 1912 AD – The famous bust of Nefertiti is discovered at Amarna.
- Modern era – The bust becomes one of the most famous images of ancient Egypt.

DID YOU KNOW?
Nefertiti’s bust is admired not only as a portrait, but also as an example of the refined artistic style of the Amarna Period.

HIDDEN DETAIL
The Amarna Period changed the way royal figures were shown in art. Nefertiti appears in scenes that emphasize the royal family’s relationship with Aten, making her image important for understanding both art and religion.

FUN FACTS

- Her bust is one of the most copied images from ancient Egypt.
- She is often remembered as a symbol of beauty, power, and royal identity.
