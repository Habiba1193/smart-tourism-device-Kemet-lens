SHORT DESCRIPTION
Tutankhamun was a young pharaoh of Egypt’s 18th Dynasty during the New Kingdom. He became world-famous because his tomb in the Valley of the Kings was discovered in 1922 with many treasures still inside, including the famous golden funerary mask.

OVERVIEW
Tutankhamun ruled ancient Egypt for a short period, but his modern importance is much larger than the length of his reign. His tomb, known as KV62, gave archaeologists one of the richest views of royal burial traditions in ancient Egypt. Unlike many royal tombs, it was found with a large number of objects still preserved, helping historians understand royal life, funerary rituals, art, jewelry, furniture, weapons, chariots, and beliefs about the afterlife.

HISTORICAL BACKGROUND
Tutankhamun belonged to the 18th Dynasty, a powerful royal line of the New Kingdom. He became king while still young, after the religious changes connected with Akhenaten and the worship of Aten. During Tutankhamun’s reign, Egypt moved back toward traditional religious practices, especially the worship of Amun and other traditional gods. He died young, and his tomb was smaller than many other royal tombs, possibly because his death was unexpected.

MATERIALS ASSOCIATED WITH ARTIFACTS
Gold, wood, stone, linen, faience, glass, precious stones, and other materials found among his funerary treasures.

FAMOUS FOR
Tutankhamun is famous for his nearly intact tomb, his golden funerary mask, and the large collection of royal objects discovered inside the tomb.

LOCATION
His tomb, KV62, is located in the Valley of the Kings near Luxor, Egypt.

SYMBOLISM
Tutankhamun’s treasures symbolize royal power, protection in the afterlife, divine kingship, and ancient Egyptian beliefs about death and rebirth. The golden mask is especially associated with eternal life, royal identity, and the transformation of the king into an immortal being.

WHY IT MATTERS
Tutankhamun is important because his tomb provided an extraordinary collection of ancient Egyptian objects in one place. These objects helped archaeologists understand how royal Egyptians prepared for the afterlife and how art, religion, and kingship were connected. His discovery also increased worldwide interest in ancient Egypt.

HISTORICAL TIMELINE
- c. 1332 BC – Tutankhamun becomes pharaoh as a young king.
- c. 1323 BC – Tutankhamun dies at a young age.
- 1922 AD – Howard Carter discovers the tomb in the Valley of the Kings.
- 1920s AD – The tomb and its treasures become famous around the world.

DID YOU KNOW?
Tutankhamun became one of the most famous pharaohs not because of long military campaigns or a long reign, but because his tomb preserved thousands of objects that revealed royal Egyptian burial traditions.

HIDDEN DETAIL
The tomb contained more than one type of object: furniture, jewelry, chariots, weapons, ritual objects, and personal items. This helps show that royal tombs were designed as a complete afterlife environment, not only as burial places.

FUN FACTS
- Tutankhamun is often called “King Tut.”
- His golden mask is one of the most recognizable symbols of ancient Egypt.
- His tomb discovery caused a global fascination with ancient Egyptian design and archaeology.
