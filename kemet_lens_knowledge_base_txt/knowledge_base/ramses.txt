SHORT DESCRIPTION
Ramses II was a powerful pharaoh of Egypt’s 19th Dynasty during the New Kingdom. He is known for his long reign, major building projects, military campaigns, and many colossal statues.

OVERVIEW
Ramses II is often called Ramses the Great because of the scale of his reign and monuments. He ruled for about 66 years and became one of ancient Egypt’s most famous kings. His monuments were built across Egypt and Nubia, including the temples at Abu Simbel and the Ramesseum. His statues and inscriptions helped project royal power and preserve his memory.

HISTORICAL BACKGROUND
Ramses II was the third king of the 19th Dynasty. He succeeded Seti I and ruled during a time of military activity, diplomacy, and monumental construction. One of the most famous events of his reign was the Battle of Kadesh against the Hittites. Although the battle’s result is debated, Ramses presented it as a great royal victory in inscriptions. Later, Egypt and the Hittites reached a major peace agreement.

MATERIALS ASSOCIATED WITH ARTIFACTS
Stone, especially sandstone and granite for statues and temples. His monuments also include carved reliefs and inscriptions.

FAMOUS FOR
Ramses II is famous for Abu Simbel, colossal statues, the Ramesseum, long reign, military campaigns, and his role in Egyptian-Hittite diplomacy.

LOCATION
Major monuments connected with Ramses II are found in places such as Abu Simbel, Luxor, Karnak, the Ramesseum, and other sites across Egypt and Nubia.

SYMBOLISM
Ramses II symbolizes royal strength, divine kingship, military power, and the ability of pharaohs to leave a lasting architectural legacy. His colossal statues were designed to impress viewers and communicate authority.

WHY IT MATTERS
Ramses II matters because his monuments are among the most visible examples of New Kingdom royal power. His reign shows how Egyptian kings used architecture, inscriptions, diplomacy, and religious imagery to strengthen their legacy.

HISTORICAL TIMELINE
- 1279 BC – Ramses II becomes pharaoh.
- c. 1274 BC – Battle of Kadesh occurs during his reign.
- 13th century BC – Major building projects, including Abu Simbel, are completed or expanded.
- 1213 BC – Ramses II dies after a long reign.

DID YOU KNOW?
The temples of Abu Simbel were moved in the 20th century to save them from the rising waters caused by the construction of the Aswan High Dam.

HIDDEN DETAIL
Ramses II’s monuments were not only religious buildings; they were also political messages. Their size, inscriptions, and statues were designed to show that the king was powerful, divinely supported, and remembered across the land.

FUN FACTS
- Ramses II is one of the best-known pharaohs in popular culture.
- He ruled longer than most ancient Egyptian kings.
- Many statues of Ramses II were placed in temples to make his image permanently visible.
